## Objective

Estimate the effect of delaying the same dinner by several hours while keeping the meal otherwise matched. Comparisons may be expressed by clock time or by time relative to sleep when the practical intervention is a delayed dinner and the outcome and observation period are sufficiently comparable.

Effects are calculated as later minus earlier. Positive values indicate a greater glucose response after later dinner.

## Primary meta-analysis

The primary meta-analysis includes:

- Enomoto 2026: dinner 1 hour versus 5 hours before sleep, Day 2.
- Nakamura 2021: dinner at 21:00 versus 18:00, Day 2.

Both studies measured 180-minute continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) glucose incremental area under the curve (iAUC) after a matched dinner. Effects are expressed as mean differences in mmol/L × min.

Enomoto Day 2 was selected because it corresponds most closely to Nakamura's analyzed Day 2 dinner; Nakamura excluded Day 1 as an adjustment period. Enomoto Days 1, 3, and 4 are sensitivity analyses.

## Effect estimates and paired data

Crossover comparisons are analyzed as paired observations. Reported paired or model-based contrasts are used when available. Condition-level standard deviations are not treated as independent-group standard deviations, and paired variance is not borrowed from another study.

Enomoto supplied the paired mean difference and paired-difference standard deviation. Values in mg/dL × min are divided by 18 to obtain mmol/L × min.

Nakamura's condition means were digitized from Figure 3B. The article reports `P < 0.001` for the same Day 2 dinner iAUC comparison and states that early and late dinner conditions were compared with paired t tests. The primary analysis treats the threshold as `P = 0.001` and uses 11 degrees of freedom to obtain the largest standard error compatible with the threshold.

## Statistical model

The primary model is a restricted maximum-likelihood random-effects model with a normal 95% confidence interval. A common-effect model is reported for comparison. A Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman interval is reported as a sensitivity analysis because only two studies are available.

Cochran's Q, tau-squared, and I-squared are reported but interpreted cautiously because they are unstable with two studies. No prediction interval, funnel plot, reporting-bias test, meta-regression, subgroup analysis, or leave-one-out analysis is planned.

## Sensitivity analyses

- Replace Enomoto Day 2 with Days 1, 3, and 4 in separate analyses.
- Recalculate the primary REML model treating Nakamura's threshold as exact two-sided `P = 0.0005` and `P = 0.0001`.

The P-threshold analyses do not claim that either value is the true P value. They assess whether the pooled result depends materially on treating the reported threshold as exactly 0.001.

## Other studies

All other eligible studies are reported individually in tables, plots, and narrative synthesis. They are not pooled when outcome definition, observation period, meal or challenge, population, comparison, or paired precision is not sufficiently compatible.

No additional covariance-based pooled analyses or standardized-mean-difference omnibus models will be fitted.
